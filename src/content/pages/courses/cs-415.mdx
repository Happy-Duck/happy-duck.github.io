## How the class runs

The course is built around one game, made by a team over the semester. Before any of it is built, the team has to say what the game is, on paper, in two documents.

The first is a **vision canvas**: one page that names the genre, the world, who the game is for, what a player should feel and when, the pillars the game cannot do without, and the systems needed to support them. Every pillar is written as something the player can do.

The second is a **technical design document**: the same game broken into systems and then into features small enough to program, each with a priority, a difficulty, and a risk. It is revised as the project moves; ours reached version 1.2 by late March.

## What ours said

Our game was Party School, a multiplayer party game set on the University of Illinois campus. These are four of its pillars, as written on the canvas.

| Pillar | The feeling it was written against |
| --- | --- |
| Run around the campus with friends to reach a minigame | Excited to be playing with friends |
| Explore the campus to find collectibles | Curious while exploring; surprise and joy on finding one |
| Take photos of squirrels in the squirrel-finding minigame | Curious, again |
| Avoid the Alma Mater in the horror minigame | Startled on spotting it |

The design document then turned those into eight systems, from player movement to saving. The racing minigame was marked a stretch goal from the start, which is the kind of decision the document exists to force early.

The game itself, and my part in it, is on [its own page](/projects/party-school/).
